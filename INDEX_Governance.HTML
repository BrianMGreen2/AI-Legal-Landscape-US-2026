AI Governance Framework

What Is AI Governance

Rules for how AI behaves, who is responsible when something goes wrong, and how problems are caught early.

The Agentic Era Changes Everything

When AI moves from answering to acting, every domain gets harder. Agents act sequentially — often without human oversight at each step.

Foundation

Domains 1–3 · Required before AI deployment
01
Prerequisite

Data Governance

  • Define data ownership, access rights, and model input rules
  • Ensure all outputs can be sourced and audited
  • Without this layer, more powerful AI produces more confident wrong answers
Agentic Shift
  • Agents write data — governance must cover what they can modify, not just access
Data OwnershipModel Input RulesAudit Trails
02
Foundation Layer

Risk Classification & Management

  • Classify use cases as low, medium, or high risk
  • Set approval thresholds and human oversight requirements per tier
Agentic Shift
  • Low-risk errors compound — step one can cascade into a high-impact outcome by step five
Risk TiersApproval ThresholdsEscalation Protocols
03
Foundation Layer

Accountability & Decision Rights

  • Document who approved each system and owns its outcomes
  • Designate a human in the loop at each consequential decision point
Agentic Shift
  • The business line that authorized the automation owns the outcome — design through deployment
RACI FrameworkHuman-in-the-LoopSteering Committee

Operations

Domains 4–7 · Continuous management in production
04
Operational

Ethics & Bias Oversight

  • Bias testing, fairness metrics, regular model reviews
  • Ongoing function — not a one-time launch check
Agentic Shift
  • Bias compounds across chains — a skew in agent one becomes systematic by agent five
Bias TestingModel Reviews
05
Operational

Security & Access Control

  • Minimum permissions per task — no more
  • Address AI-specific risks: prompt injection, unauthorized tool use
Agentic Shift
  • Exploited agents act in the real world — not just output text
Permission ScopingPrompt Injection
06
Operational

Transparency & Explainability

  • Explain what any AI system did and why — to regulators and stakeholders
  • A regulatory requirement and an operational necessity
Agentic Shift
  • Longer chains make explainability harder — design for it from day one
Decision RationaleRegulatory Reporting
07
Operational

Monitoring & Auditability

  • Track AI behavior in production — governance does not end at launch
  • Monitor for output drift, scope creep, and threshold calibration
Agentic Shift
  • Drift happens through accumulated small decisions — monitor behavior, not just errors
Drift DetectionIncident Logging

Compliance & Legal

Domain 8 · The framework that makes governance enforceable
08
Compliance

Legal & Regulatory Compliance

  • Contracts, liability allocation, industry regulations, internal audit
  • Legal counsel is the partner that makes governance enforceable and defensible
Agentic Shift
  • Automated decision liability is the fastest-growing AI legal risk — proactive partnership builds defensible ground now
Contracts & LiabilityIndustry RegulationsInternal Audit
Legal as a Governance Partner
  • Legal translates governance decisions into enforceable frameworks
  • Early involvement — at design, not just review — prevents the costliest exposures
  • The strongest programs treat legal as a strategic partner across all nine domains

Content & Asset Rights

Domain 9 · For organizations whose IP is consumed by AI
09
Emerging Domain

Content & Asset Rights Governance

  • Domains 1–8 govern AI deployers — Domain 9 governs IP holders whose content is consumed by others' AI
  • Establish rights, compensation, and accountability for what others' AI does with your assets
Licensing Framework
  • Ratify access tiers and compensation internally before any negotiation
Telemetry & Audit Rights
  • Require contractual rights to verify usage — without telemetry, compensation is unenforceable
Derivative Use Policy
  • Define permissible AI-derived outputs — synthesis and summarization require explicit contract language
Agentic Shift
  • Agents obscure content sources across multi-step processes — rights evolve faster than standards
